# Brand identity and adaptation contract

System version: **0.1.0-alpha.1 · prerelease**. Brand identity defines recognition, voice and storytelling. Functional UI contracts define interaction mechanics. The layers connect but must not be conflated.

## Canonical naming

Surface Field is the established DesignA11y element name; machine key surfaceField. Sensory describes its purpose. Resolve the informal phrase sensory field to Surface Field rather than creating a second element. Tactile background assets remain distinct image/surface resources.

## Context first

- What is the brand mission, intention and point of view?
- Who are its audiences and what contexts/access needs matter?
- What should people recognize, feel or understand?
- Which channels, languages and assets are in scope?

## Logo systems

Contract key: `logoSystems`.

- Which primary mark, wordmark, symbol, secondary, monochrome and small-size variants exist?
- Where may each appear, at what minimum size and with what clear space?
- What proportions, placement, backgrounds and alterations are prohibited?
- Which files are owned, licensed or pending, and what is the fallback if a mark is unavailable?

Required outputs: asset slots and source rights; variant-to-context rules; minimum size and clear space; background/monochrome pairings; do/don’t examples; accessible naming and decorative-use rules.

DesignA11y reference: Original wordmark retained; clean brand symbol 40:15; notched favicon 11:16. Asset-specific clear-space/minimum-size review remains required.

## Brand color palette

Contract key: `brandPalette`.

- What are the primary, secondary and accent signature colors?
- What emotional, cultural or symbolic associations are intended, and in which contexts?
- Which combinations, proportions and backgrounds express the brand?
- How do brand colors map separately to UI text, surface, action, error, warning, success and focus roles?

Required outputs: brand palette and intended associations; usage proportions and pairings; functional semantic mapping; light/dark alternatives; contrast evidence per actual state; non-color meaning cues.

DesignA11y reference: Aubergine, Petal, Blush, Rose and Copper remain the reference anchors. Do not infer universal emotional effects or substitute accents for validated status colors.

## Brand typography and display

Contract key: `brandTypography`.

- Which display and supporting families express the identity?
- What hierarchy, weight, tracking, case and typesetting give it personality?
- What reading/UI fonts, language coverage and fallbacks are required?
- What licenses apply, and where must specialty display type be restricted?

Required outputs: display/reading/UI role mapping; type scale and composite styles; font licensing and fallbacks; language and glyph support; channel-specific examples; reflow and long-content evidence.

DesignA11y reference: Anton display and Archivo reusable body/UI; live-site Proxima Nova mapping is separate. Original vector wordmark is not regenerated by changing a font.

## Graphic language and motifs

Contract key: `graphicLanguage`.

- What signature shapes, patterns, dividers, frames or composition devices create recognition?
- What meaning, hierarchy and frequency should they carry?
- How do they adapt across web, social, print and motion?
- What should be absent or restrained, and where are quiet reading zones?

Required outputs: motif inventory and purpose; geometry and composition rules; placement and frequency; background/foreground relationships; responsive and channel adaptations; exclusions and sensory intensity limits.

DesignA11y reference: Frame, seam and Surface Field have distinct roles. They are DesignA11y expressions; downstream brands may replace or omit them.

## Iconography and illustration

Contract key: `iconographyIllustration`.

- Does the brand need a proprietary illustration world, functional icons, or both?
- What stroke, corner, fill, perspective, detail and optical scale rules apply?
- What subjects, metaphors and representational choices fit the brand?
- Which rights-cleared asset sources and accessibility naming rules apply?

Required outputs: brand illustration direction; functional-icon conventions kept distinct; stroke/fill/detail specifications; optical sizing and asset slots; inclusive representation rules; meaning, alt and decorative-use guidance.

DesignA11y reference: Original functional UI icon drafts exist. A full brand illustration world is not yet defined; do not pretend generic UI icons establish that direction.

## Photography and art direction

Contract key: `photographyArtDirection`.

- What subjects, settings, casting and representation support the brand mission?
- What lighting, composition, lens/perspective, color grading and retouching rules apply?
- What image and background references are useful, and exactly what should be learned from each?
- Are tactile/sensory surfaces appropriate for this brand; which texture, finish, density, intensity and exclusions fit?

Required outputs: photography and background directions separately; reference annotations and rights; casting and representation; lighting/composition/grading; image purpose, crop and text-safe zones; sensory surface brief and quality rubric.

DesignA11y reference: Continuous tactile fields are established; smooth/rough/spiky and glossy/matte/velvet/sanded variety is allowed. Isolated abstract objects are excluded for the surface layer. A photography/casting direction remains to be defined or intentionally marked not applicable.

## Voice and tone

Contract key: `voiceTone`.

- What personality, point of view and communication principles should people recognize?
- How should tone change across editorial, marketing, help, errors and social?
- What vocabulary, spelling, terminology, naming and phrases are preferred or prohibited?
- How should the brand discuss disability, AI, uncertainty and accessibility claims?

Required outputs: voice principles and vocabulary; channel/context tone matrix; preferred/prohibited language; before/after examples; inclusive/plain-language guidance; evidence and claim boundaries.

DesignA11y reference: Inclusive design, accessibility, lived experience and AI define the content context. Formal vocabulary and channel-tone matrices need an explicit brand-owner decision; generic designer samples must not replace the context.

## Brand motion and animation

Contract key: `brandMotion`.

- What pacing, rhythm, easing and kinetic personality express the identity?
- Which signature transitions or graphic movements belong to brand storytelling?
- How do video/social motion and functional interface feedback differ?
- What static/reduced-motion alternatives, interruption, flashing and intensity constraints apply?

Required outputs: brand kinetic principles; timing/easing tokens; signature storytelling recipes; channel delivery/export guidance; functional motion contracts separately; static/preference alternatives and review evidence.

DesignA11y reference: Frame, seam and field expression plus finite interaction recipes are documented. Brand storytelling does not authorize autoplay or continuous UI motion; focus remains static.

## Cross-channel expression

Use native Spec 123:1319 and Brand / Expression & applications page 120:2. Ask which touchpoints matter and how the mark, motif grammar, backgrounds, voice and composition adapt across packaging, social, video, print and digital experiences. Define repeat density, scale, spacing, crop, orientation, quiet zones, reference intent and prohibited transformations. References inform structure; never copy their artwork. Figma-first guided workflow: 125:2.

## Decision and application rules

- Ask about every category; do not infer that missing answers mean approval.
- Allow not applicable with a reason and a safe fallback; do not force photography or sensory materials onto every brand.
- For references ask what to borrow conceptually and what to reject; reference access is not redistribution permission.
- Separate brand expression from functional UI semantics and accessibility behavior.
- Create a reviewable profile with field-level source, decision owner, status and rationale before applying it to a copy.
- Apply approved choices centrally through theme variables/styles/asset slots; keep stable component APIs.
- Revalidate accessibility after adaptation; inherited rules do not certify the outcome.
- Keep private reference materials and source permissions out of distributable copies.

Each decision records value, purpose, source, owner, status (approved/proposed/pending/not-applicable), examples, exclusions, token/style/asset mappings and required validation. Replacing identity must not silently change semantics, keyboard behavior, focus visibility, status meaning, preference alternatives or evidence requirements.
