# Cross-channel brand expression

System version: **0.1.0-alpha.1 · prerelease exploration**.

The brand system extends beyond web UI. Recognition should survive across packaging, social, video, print and other chosen touchpoints through a shared visual grammar. These studies are original DesignA11y work; reference projects inform how to organize the system, not artwork to reproduce.

## Expression layers

1. **Identity assets:** original wordmark, clean symbol, favicon and permitted monochrome/size/context variants. Preserve source geometry; replace a brand asset centrally when adapting.
2. **Graphic grammar:** the symbol may repeat, the open frame may echo, and a quiet seam may establish cadence. Control scale, spacing, density, orientation, crop and frequency. Record permitted transformations; do not distort marks.
3. **Background language:** graphic motifs, tactile surfaces, photography and illustration are distinct families. A repeated logo is a graphic pattern; a tactile surface is a continuous material field with a sensory role. Do not substitute one simply because both fill a background.
4. **Composition:** establish message hierarchy, quiet text zones, alignment and the relationship between mark, headline and image field. Recompose for a format rather than stretching a whole layout.
5. **Channel behavior:** adapt delivery and production requirements while retaining recognizable identity. Static layouts do not establish runtime, print or video readiness.

These are draft expression options. Repetition is a controlled parameter, not a requirement to fill every available area. Avoid visual monotony, overbranding, high-frequency interference and decoration that obscures meaning.

## Adaptation interview

- Which applications matter: website/app, packaging, print, social posts/stories, video, email, signage, events or other environments?
- Should marks or symbol geometry generate repeat patterns, frames, dividers or other motifs? Which transformations preserve recognition?
- What pattern density, rhythm, scale, spacing, orientation, crop, contrast and quiet zones fit each context?
- Which signature backgrounds are graphic motifs, tactile surfaces, photography or illustration, and what purpose does each serve?
- What message hierarchy, information density, aspect ratios and production constraints apply to each channel?
- Which expressions remain recognizable without the logo, and which require the primary mark?
- What should vary by campaign or asset, and what must stay stable?
- Are print production, captions/transcripts, alt text, safe zones or accessible digital equivalents required?

The agent outputs shared motif grammar and prohibited transformations; pattern and background families; cross-channel composition principles; channel-specific template/asset mappings; format, crop, density and quiet-zone rules; production handoff and validation requirements; examples of consistent variation, not identical scaled layouts. Record source, owner, rationale and approved/proposed/pending/not-applicable status for every decision. Apply identity changes centrally; keep accessibility behavior and evidence gates explicit.
